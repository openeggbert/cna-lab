READ THIS FIRST

This is not a menu-driven pet collector

You do not select an adult creature from a list. You begin with an egg, build a care history, and discover the result as it grows. Food, games, sleep, discipline, messes, illness, and missed calls all matter.

The game continues to account for time after you close it. A short visit is enough when your pet is well, but ignoring it for too long has consequences.

Your first session

  1. Open CnaTamagotchi.
  2. Wait for the egg to hatch; this takes about five minutes.
  3. Use Food after the hatch, then check Status.
  4. Before leaving, clean any mess and turn off the light if your pet is asleep.

THE FULL WALKTHROUGH

24 lessons for a healthy first generation

Read in order for your first pet, or jump to a lesson when you need an answer. Every lesson describes the player-facing behaviour of the device rather than implementation details.

01

Meet the device

The shell has eight care icons around a 32 × 16 monochrome LCD. The display is intentionally tiny: black pixels are on; olive-green pixels are off. The creature and its needs fit into that constraint.

02

Learn A, B, and C

A moves forward. B opens, confirms, or performs an action. C backs out of a screen. On a computer use A/→, B/Enter/Space, and C/Backspace/Escape. You can also click or tap the three round buttons below the LCD.

03

Understand selection

On the home screen, one shell icon is darker than the others. That is the current selection. Press A until the icon you want is selected, then press B. The icons are not separate modern buttons; the three-button rhythm is the intended way to play.

04

Wait for the hatch

A new generation begins as an egg. It hatches after roughly five minutes. There is nothing to feed before the hatch, so use this time to become comfortable with the selection ring and the Status icon.

05

Open Food

Food is the first icon in the top row. Press B to open it. The LCD shows MEAL and SNACK; press A to move the pointer, B to serve the highlighted option, or C to leave without serving anything.

06

Choose a Meal when hungry

A Meal restores one hunger segment and adds a small amount of weight. It is the normal answer to an empty hunger meter or a genuine hunger call. Feeding at sensible intervals is better than waiting for a crisis.

07

Use Snacks carefully

A Snack restores one happiness segment, but adds more weight than a Meal. Snacks are useful when happiness is low and a game is not practical. Repeated snacks at an unhealthy weight can cause illness, so they are not a substitute for play.

08

Read the home meters

The three groups across the top of the home LCD are hunger, happiness, and discipline. Each group contains four two-pixel segments. More black segments mean a fuller meter. Empty hunger or happiness eventually demands attention.

09

Use Status deliberately

Select Status and press B. Repeated presses of B cycle through age/weight, hunger/happiness, discipline/care mistakes, and the active line/game profile. Press C to return home. Status is your calm check-in screen, not an emergency screen.

10

Learn the active line

The fourth Status page reads PEEK or NUM. PEEK belongs to the Pipple line. NUM belongs to the Budbit line. The line is determined by the current generation; it changes which mini-game the Game icon opens.

11

Play PEEK

For a Pipple-line pet, select Game. The LCD shows two positions. Press A to choose left or right, then press B to reveal the result. A win raises happiness and helps control weight. Merely opening the game does not grant its benefits.

12

Play NUM

For a Budbit-line pet, select Game. The LCD presents a number and an up/down choice. Press A to choose whether the next number will be lower or higher, then press B to resolve the round. Winning has the same care benefit as PEEK.

13

Know when games are unavailable

A sleeping pet will not play. If you try an unavailable action, the home LCD briefly shows a small cross instead of changing the pet. This is a reminder to solve the current need first, not an error that damages the pet.

14

Notice sleep

From the child stage onward, the pet follows a day/night schedule. A sleeping pet is marked with a small sleep symbol. It rests at night and wakes in the morning. Its light is a care responsibility, not a decorative setting.

15

Turn off the light

When the pet falls asleep, select Light and press B to turn the light off. Waking turns it back on automatically. Leaving the light on creates a genuine attention call. Pressing Light while the pet is awake has no effect.

16

Clean every mess

Waste appears as a small mark near the lower-left part of the home LCD. The Clean icon removes it. Do not wait for several messes: uncleaned waste contributes to poor health and can make the pet sick.

17

Recognise illness

A small cross on the LCD means illness. Select Medicine and press B. Medicine is for illness only; it will not restore hunger, happiness, or discipline. If there is no illness, the device gives the unavailable-action cross instead.

18

Respond to attention calls

A square attention marker appears at the upper-right of the LCD, and the Attention shell icon becomes dark. Calls can be caused by empty hunger, empty happiness, a sleeping pet with its light on, or a deliberate false call. Genuine calls should be handled within about fifteen minutes.

19

Use Discipline only for a false call

Sometimes a pet calls even though food, happiness, sleep, and cleanliness are fine. This is the correct time for Discipline. It raises discipline only for that false-call situation. Using it at other times does nothing, so do not use it as a generic punishment button.

20

Avoid care mistakes

Ignoring a genuine call creates a care mistake. Care mistakes remain part of the pet's history and influence evolution. The Status screen shows their count. There is no single perfect adult button: consistent sensible care produces better outcomes.

21

Watch weight and health

Meals add a little weight, Snacks add more, and a won game reduces it. Extremely high weight combined with more Snacks can lead to illness. Severe neglect, illness, and uncleaned waste reduce health; health reaching zero leads to Farewell.

22

Follow growth

The egg hatches at about 5 minutes, becomes a child at about 65 minutes, reaches teen around day 3, and adulthood around day 6. Each line has a baby, child, two teen outcomes, six regular adults, and a rare late form.

23

Close the game safely

The game saves a new slot once, then saves after real care changes. It does not rewrite an unchanged save simply because you opened and closed the window. On the next launch, elapsed time is applied safely, including incomplete minutes.

24

Begin the next generation

If health reaches zero, the pet enters Farewell. The LCD displays NEW. Press B to begin a clean new egg. The next line is selected deterministically from the saved generation seed, so the new life has its own path and game profile.

HELP

Frequently asked questions

Why did an icon show a small cross instead of changing anything?

The action was unavailable: for example Medicine without illness, Light while the pet is awake, Game while it is asleep, or Discipline without a false call. The pet was not harmed and the unchanged save was not rewritten.

Where is the save file?

New slots use your standard per-user data folder: LOCALAPPDATA on Windows, ~/Library/Application Support on macOS, or XDG_DATA_HOME / ~/.local/share on Linux. An existing saves/slot-1.json next to the game, or its .bak backup, remains in that location and takes precedence.

What if the save cannot be read?

The LCD shows ERR. If a valid backup exists, REST is selected first; press B to restore it. Press A to select NEW, then B to archive the damaged file as slot-1.json.corrupt and begin a fresh egg. If no valid backup exists, only NEW is shown. Nothing is replaced automatically.

Can I choose a specific adult?

No. Adults are care outcomes. The new generation determines its Pipple or Budbit line, while care history determines its teen and adult outcome.

Can I pause time?

Not in the default experience. CNA Tamagotchi is designed as a real-time pet. Check it before extended absences, especially once it has started receiving calls.

Can I change the LCD colour?

The authentic default is olive. If you launch the desktop executable from a command line, add --lcd-palette=amber, --lcd-palette=ice, or --lcd-palette=mono. This changes only the physical LCD colours; the display is always 32 × 16 and one-bit.
